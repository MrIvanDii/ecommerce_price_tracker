      The History of Gold Coins

      Gold coins have a long and rich history and have been valued since ancient times for their rarity, durability, and universal appeal as symbols of wealth and power. First minted by the Lydians around 600 BCE, they are one of the oldest and most trusted mediums of exchange. Today, gold coins are prized not only for their historical significance but also as a reliable store of value and a hedge against economic uncertainty.

      The Benefits of Gold Coins

      Gold coins are a convenient way to own physical gold and are manufactured by leading mints across the world to meet strong demand. They are available in a wide variety of sizes and weights to suit many different financial goals and budgets, making them a highly flexible option.

      Gold UK coins, such as Sovereigns and Britannias, particularly appeal to buyers in the UK, as their status as British legal tender exempts them from Capital Gains Tax (CGT). All gold coins are also free from VAT (Value Added Tax) for taxpayers in the UK. These tax efficiencies can often mean substantial savings.

      While gold coins may sometimes have a slightly higher premium than gold bars, they can offer long-term value through their liquidity, portability, and universally recognised designs. Their distinct appearances also make them easy to identify worldwide, making them highly desirable among both collectors and bullion buyers.

      Gold coins are easy to buy and sell, giving owners quick access to cash when needed. Their small size offers great flexibility, allowing individuals to sell part of their collection as they wish to, without having to liquidate their entire holding all at once. Their tangible form also provides a sense of security that digital assets may lack, making gold coins a popular choice for individuals seeking to manage their wealth own effectively.

      Gold Coins FAQs

      • The value of a gold coin is determined by its weight, fineness (purity), and the current market price of gold. Most gold coins are typically minted in troy ounces, with common weights being 1oz, 1/2oz, 1/4oz, and 1/10oz. The price of gold constantly fluctuates based on market conditions, so it is important to check the current spot price to find out the value of your gold coin.

        In addition to the value of the gold within the coin, some sought-after gold coins can carry additional collectable value, sometimes making them worth a little more than their gold content alone due to factors such as rarity, demand, or condition. These can include coins such as Victorian Shield Sovereigns, Proof Gold Coins, and Graded Coins.

      • Gold coins can be a good investment for several reasons. They offer a tangible asset that can act as a hedge against inflation and economic uncertainty, as gold has historically maintained its value over time. Gold coins can provide a physical form of wealth that can be easily stored and secured.

        Some gold coins, especially those that are British legal tender like British Sovereigns and Britannias, may be exempt from capital gains tax (CGT) in the UK, potentially providing tax advantages for collectors. Their liquidity also allows for easy buying and selling.

        However, as with any investment, it is important to consider your financial goals carry out your own research before making a decision.

      • At Atkinsons Bullion & Coins, we offer a wide selection of 24 and 22 carat gold bullion coins, including well-known products like the UK Sovereign, UK Britannia, South African Krügerrand, Canadian Maple Leaf, and USA Eagle. We proudly stock coins from leading mints like The Royal Mint and Canadian Mint, ensuring quality and trust for our clients.

        The UK Britannia: First issued in 1987, the Britannia is an iconic UK gold bullion coin. Its status as legal tender means it is exempt from Capital Gains Tax, making it a popular choice. The one-ounce Britannia has a face value of £100, with fractional sizes available, and features a design that symbolises strength and protection.

        The UK Sovereign: Dating back to 1489, the Sovereign is a historic gold coin that remains a symbol of British currency. As a legal tender coin, it is also exempt from Capital Gains Tax. Struck in 22ct gold by The Royal Mint, the coin is famous for featuring Benedetto Pistrucci’s iconic St George and the Dragon design, although there have also been some alternative commemorative designs throughout the years.

        The South African Krugerrand: Minted since 1967, the Krugerrand is recognized as the world’s first bullion gold coin and remains popular among collectors. Made of 22-carat gold, it features Paul Kruger on the obverse and a springbok on the reverse.

        The Canadian Maple Leaf: Introduced in 1979, this coin is celebrated for its 999.9 purity and iconic maple leaf design, representing Canada’s national identity. The one-ounce Maple Leaf has a face value of CAD $50.

        The American Eagle: Since 1986, the American Eagle has showcased the classic design of Lady Liberty. As a 22-carat gold coin, it is guaranteed by the U.S. government to contain the stated amount of actual gold in troy ounces, with all gold coming from sources within the USA.

If you are looking to make a larger purchase and prefer a lower premium, gold bars may be more suitable.
